Bushfires and Air Pollution in Australia

Australia ranks first globally in bushfire activity. This visualisation explores fire hotspots, their geographic distribution, and the impact on air quality across the country.

Section 1 — Global Context

1. Global Bushfire Activity

Bushfires are a global crisis. The map below shows recent fire hotspots detected around the world using NASA VIIRS satellite data.

2. Global Bushfire Activity by Country

Australia recorded the highest number of high-confidence fire detections globally, ahead of Russia and Mexico.

Section 2 — Australia's Bushfire Landscape

3. Bushfire Locations in Australia

This dot map shows detected bushfire locations across Australia from March to May 2026.

4. Fire Intensity Across Australia

This proportional symbol map shows fire intensity using Fire Radiative Power (FRP). Larger and darker circles indicate stronger fire activity.

5. Bushfire Detections by State

Western Australia and the Northern Territory recorded the highest number of fire detections.

6. Total Fire Detections by State

Western Australia alone accounted for over 88,000 fire detections — more than double all other states combined.

Section 3 — Fire Activity Over Time

7. Daily Bushfire Detections in Australia

Fire activity peaked on 7 May 2026 with 8,154 detections in a single day — a 16-fold increase from early March levels.

8. Daily Fire Detections by State

Each panel shows the daily fire trend for one state. Western Australia dominates throughout the period.

9. Monthly Fire Detections by State

This heatmap shows how fire activity varied across states and months. Western Australia's April surge is the most severe episode of the period.

Section 4 — Air Pollution Impact

10. Monthly Average PM2.5 by Station

PM2.5 levels across Victorian monitoring stations peak in autumn months (March–May), coinciding with peak bushfire season. White cells indicate missing data due to equipment maintenance.

11. Daily PM2.5 Levels Across Victoria

Daily PM2.5 readings from EPA Victoria monitoring stations throughout 2024 show elevated pollution levels during bushfire-prone periods. Hover over the lines to explore individual station readings.
